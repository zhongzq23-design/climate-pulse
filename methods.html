Methods & definitions

Climate Pulse publishes operational definitions so readers can see how events, mapped footprints, exposure indicators and climate-context metrics are constructed. Exposure means spatial or economic screening; it does not mean verified damage.

3× daily monitoring Mapped GDACS footprints FAO crop area NASA MODIS land cover World Bank WDI CRU-TS v4.10 · 1901–2025 Context ≠ attribution

1. Event monitoring

The Standard Event backend checks NASA EONET, GDACS and Copernicus CEMS Rapid Mapping approximately three times per day, at 00:17, 08:17 and 16:17 UTC. Source records are normalized into a common event structure and archived before publication on the map.

  • NASA EONET — event discovery and source metadata.
  • GDACS — disaster records, alert levels and hazard geometry.
  • Copernicus CEMS — human-triggered emergency mapping activations.
A record appearing on Climate Pulse means that it passed the current operational rules. It does not mean that Climate Pulse independently verifies every impact statement in the source record.

2. Deduplication and map clustering

Source IDs and links are retained. Obvious cross-source duplicates may be merged when they describe the same hazard type and occur close together in space and time.

candidate duplicate if: same hazard type AND distance < 80 km AND |date₁ − date₂| ≤ 5 days

Major wildfire records may also be grouped visually when they occur within roughly 180 km and 7 days. A grouped marker is a display object rather than one source polygon.

3. Mapped event footprint

When a usable GDACS polygon is available for wildfire, tropical cyclone, drought or flood, Climate Pulse shows a regional footprint map at the start of expanded details. Country boundaries, coastlines, selected cities and latitude/longitude are orientation aids.

The blue browser outline is a topology-preserving simplified copy. All exposure calculations use the unsimplified mapped polygon, not the simplified display geometry.

browser footprint = simplify(mapped source polygon)
If no usable source polygon exists, no footprint panel is shown. Climate Pulse does not invent a generic polygon merely to fill the map.

4. Wildfire exposure

The main-map gate for GDACS wildfire records is burned area ≥ 10,000 ha. Where a usable fire polygon exists, the existing population screening uses JRC GHSL GHS-WUP-POP R2025A at approximately 1 km resolution.

Direct population = P(F)
Nearby ≤5 km = P(F ⊕ 5 km) − P(F)

Climate Pulse also estimates the forest area spatially overlapping the mapped wildfire footprint using the NASA MODIS MCD12C1 Version 6.1 2024 0.05° land-cover product. Forest is the summed sub-pixel percentage of IGBP classes 1–5.

Forest overlap = Σ cell area × forest fraction inside F
Forest overlap is not confirmed forest loss. A mapped fire/event polygon can include unburned patches and the MODIS product is a land-cover reference, not a fire-severity product.

5. Tropical cyclones and storms

A cyclone centre point is not treated as the impact area. GDACS tropical-cyclone wind/impact polygons are preferred. Orange/Red GDACS storms remain visible; Green records require populated footprint exposure. EONET-only storms can use a labelled 300 km population proximity screen when a wind footprint is unavailable.

Where a mapped GDACS cyclone footprint exists, Climate Pulse also calculates a potential GDP exposure proxy using the country-split footprint, GHSL 2025 population and World Bank World Development Indicators GDP per capita for 2024.

GDP exposure proxy = Σcountry [GHSL population in TC footprint × WDI 2024 GDP per capita]
This is not economic loss. It is a transparent macroeconomic exposure proxy, not gridded local GDP, asset value, insured loss, business interruption or observed damage.

6. Drought land, forest and crop exposure

Climate Pulse no longer displays a drought population-exposure count. For a GDACS drought with a mapped polygon D, the public view instead emphasizes potentially exposed area.

Mapped footprintGeodesic area of the unsimplified GDACS drought polygon.
Land areaMODIS 2024 sub-pixel land area, using 100% minus IGBP water fraction.
Forest areaMODIS 2024 IGBP forest classes 1–5 overlapping the polygon.
Crop areaFAO CROPGRIDS 2020 crop physical area overlapping the polygon.
Potential forest exposure = Σ cell area × MODIS forest fraction inside D
Potential crop exposure = Σ CROPGRIDS crop physical area inside D

FAO CROPGRIDS provides 173 crops at 0.05° resolution for 2020 and distinguishes harvested area from crop physical area. Climate Pulse uses crop physical area, because the intended quantity is land occupied by crops rather than the number of harvesting cycles.

These are spatial overlaps, not impacts. They do not measure crop failure, yield loss, forest mortality, water shortage, food insecurity or drought severity. CROPGRIDS can also overstate distinct cropland in some multi-cropping situations when crop-specific physical areas are summed.

7. Flood GDP exposure

When GDACS provides a usable flood polygon, Climate Pulse can calculate the same country-split World Bank/GHSL GDP exposure proxy used for tropical cyclones.

GDP exposure proxy = Σcountry [GHSL 2025 population in flood footprint × WDI 2024 GDP per capita]

Using country GDP per capita makes the method globally reproducible and current, but it cannot represent within-country concentration of economic activity. The label therefore remains potential GDP exposure proxy.

8. Reference datasets for exposure

PopulationJRC GHSL GHS-WUP-POP R2025A, epoch 2025, approximately 1 km authoritative source.
Land / forestNASA MODIS MCD12C1.061, 2024, 0.05°, IGBP sub-pixel class percentages.
CropsFAO CROPGRIDS v1.08, 2020, 0.05°, 173 crops; crop physical area.
GDPWorld Bank WDI NY.GDP.PCAP.CD, 2024 common-year GDP per capita in current US$.

For MODIS, land area is 100% minus the IGBP water fraction; forest is classes 1–5. NASA cautions users when interpreting recent MCD12 land-cover years because the supervised-classification training database was not continuously updated and some training sites may have changed land cover. Climate Pulse therefore treats MODIS as a broad land/forest exposure reference rather than exact contemporary parcel mapping.

9. Event climate context

The public view uses annual climate context only. Climate background is extracted from the nearest valid land cell in CRU-TS v4.10, a 0.5° global land dataset covering 1901–2025. If a reported coordinate is offshore, the backend may use the nearest valid CRU land cell within 500 km and reports that distance.

DroughtTemperature + precipitation + VPD
Wildfire / HeatTemperature + precipitation + VPD
Flood / Storm / LandslideTemperature + precipitation

Monthly anomalies and near-real-time weather are not used in the current public climate panel.

10. Long-term annual indicators

Each event keeps the full 1901–2025 annual series for its selected variables. The public summary includes an early baseline, a recent ten-year mean and an ordinary least-squares long-term trend.

Early baseline1901–1930 annual mean.
Recent annual mean2016–2025 annual mean.
Long-term trendOLS slope for 1901–2025, reported per century.
Change = mean(2016–2025) − mean(1901–1930)
Trend = OLS slope(annual value ~ year) × 100 years

No significance badge or p-value is displayed. Charts retain annual values and a 5-year moving mean for readability.

11. CRU VPD calculation

CRU provides monthly actual vapour pressure (AVP) as vap. Saturation vapour pressure (SVP) is calculated from monthly mean temperature T; VPD is calculated before annual aggregation.

VPD = SVP − AVP
SVP = 6.1078 × exp(aT / (T + b))   [hPa]

For T ≥ 0°C, a = 17.269, b = 237.3; for T < 0°C, a = 21.875, b = 265.5.

Temporal-resolution limitation: monthly-mean temperature cannot reproduce the diurnal temperature cycle or sub-daily asymmetric warming. This is a long-term climate-context metric, not high-frequency VPD.

See Zhong, Z., Chen, H.W., Dai, A. et al. (2025), “Sub-diurnal asymmetric warming has amplified atmospheric dryness since the 1980s,” Nature Communications 16, 8247 ↗.

12. Climate context is not attribution

A warming, drying or VPD trend can provide useful local background information, but it does not by itself demonstrate that anthropogenic climate change caused a particular event.

Climate Pulse keeps event detection, mapped footprint, asset/population exposure, local climate context and formal event attribution as separate concepts.

13. Main limitations

  • Hazard polygons and event coordinates can change as an event evolves.
  • Browser footprints are simplified; exposure calculations use unsimplified source geometry.
  • Forest/crop overlap does not equal ecological damage, crop loss or yield loss.
  • MODIS recent-year land-cover classifications require caution and are used only as broad exposure context.
  • The GDP indicator is a country-level per-capita proxy and cannot represent within-country spatial economic concentration.
  • Gridded residential population is not real-time human presence.
  • Grouped-event member sums can double-count overlapping areas.
  • CRU is land-only; offshore events may use the nearest valid land cell.
  • CRU-derived VPD from monthly means omits sub-daily temperature variability.
