Methods & definitions

Climate Pulse follows a source-first policy. When GDACS or its upstream hazard system already publishes a usable metric, that value is preferred. Climate Pulse calculates an equivalent metric only when the source value is unavailable, and labels the result as a derived estimate. Supplemental metrics are kept distinct from source impacts.

1. Metric authority and terminology

The same variable is not calculated twice by default. Climate Pulse uses this order:

structured GDACS / upstream metric → Climate Pulse fallback estimate → optional supplemental context
Exposed populationPeople spatially inside a modelled hazard footprint or buffer. This does not imply harm.
Affected populationUsed only when a source reports an actual impact. It is not inferred from a polygon overlay.
Displaced populationUsed only for source-reported displacement or evacuation-type impact data.
Estimated population exposedA Climate Pulse-derived spatial estimate used only when the upstream equivalent is unavailable.
A source metric may itself be modelled. “Source-first” means Climate Pulse preserves the upstream authority and semantics; it does not mean the value is an observed headcount.

2. Event monitoring

The Standard Event backend checks NASA EONET, GDACS and Copernicus CEMS Rapid Mapping approximately three times per day, at 00:17, 08:17 and 16:17 UTC. Source records are normalized into a common event structure and archived before publication on the map.

  • NASA EONET — event discovery and source metadata.
  • GDACS — disaster records, alert levels, hazard geometry and hazard-specific impact/exposure resources.
  • Copernicus CEMS — human-triggered emergency mapping activations.
A record appearing on Climate Pulse means that it passed the current operational rules. It does not mean Climate Pulse independently verifies every source statement.

3. Deduplication and map clustering

Source IDs and links are retained. Obvious cross-source duplicates may be merged when they describe the same hazard type and occur close together in space and time.

candidate duplicate if: same hazard type AND distance < 80 km AND |date₁ − date₂| ≤ 5 days

Major wildfire records may also be grouped visually when they occur within roughly 180 km and 7 days. A grouped marker is a display object rather than one source polygon. Member sums may overlap and are never labelled as unique-population totals.

4. Mapped event footprint

When a usable GDACS polygon is available for wildfire, tropical cyclone, drought or flood, Climate Pulse shows a regional footprint map in expanded details. The browser outline is a topology-preserving simplified copy.

All Climate Pulse-derived exposure calculations use the unsimplified mapped source polygon, not the browser-simplified geometry.

browser footprint = simplify(mapped source polygon)
If no usable source polygon exists, no footprint panel is invented merely to fill the interface.

5. Wildfire: GDACS/GWIS first

For a GDACS wildfire, Climate Pulse first reads the current episode's properties.impacts[].resource.impact resource. GDACS identifies the wildfire source as GWIS. The population figures are modelled exposure, not observed harm.

POPAFFECTEDPopulation exposed in the burned area; equivalent to SUMPOP0.0.
SUMPOP1.0Population within 1 km of the burned area.
SUMPOP2.0Population within 2 km.
SUMPOP5.0Population within 5 km.
SUMPOP10.0Population within 10 km.

If POPAFFECTED or the 5-km value is unavailable, Climate Pulse can fall back to the mapped wildfire polygon and JRC GHSL 2025. The derived value is explicitly labelled as a Climate Pulse estimate and never silently replaces a source value.

fallback exposed population = GHSL population inside unsimplified GDACS fire geometry

For the public map, Orange and Red GDACS wildfires remain visible. A Green wildfire must satisfy both:

burned area ≥ 10,000 ha AND population within 5 km > 10,000

MODIS forest overlap is retained only as supplemental spatial context. It is not presented as confirmed forest loss.

6. Tropical cyclones: advisory exposure first

For GDACS tropical cyclones, Climate Pulse reads the current episode's advisory timeline resource and prefers the current observed track entry. The ambiguous GDACS pop field is not used as a headline metric.

pop39Population in the wind field of 39 kt or more.
pop74Population in the wind field of 74 kt or more.
popstormsurgePopulation in the modelled storm-surge zone.
actual / currentDescribes the advisory track position; the population exposure itself remains modelled.

Only when the structured advisory exposure is unavailable does Climate Pulse use a GDACS wind/impact polygon × GHSL fallback. EONET-only storms may use a clearly labelled 300-km proximity screen. Orange/Red GDACS storms remain visible.

A World Bank/GHSL GDP exposure proxy may also be shown, but it is a supplemental Climate Pulse metric, not a replacement for GDACS human-exposure fields and not observed economic loss.

7. Drought: risk information, not crop-loss claims

GDACS drought classification draws on the Global Drought Observatory (GDO) and its agricultural drought risk/impact framework. When GDACS publishes an agricultural drought impact/risk area in the event description, Climate Pulse displays that source value first.

The label is deliberately “Agricultural drought potential-impact/risk area”. It must not be rewritten as crop-loss area, failed-crop area or confirmed agricultural damage.

GDACS does not provide the same structured impacts[] population mapping for drought that it provides for wildfire and tropical cyclone. Climate Pulse therefore does not manufacture an “affected population” from the event polygon.

MODIS land/forest overlap and FAO CROPGRIDS crop physical area remain available as supplemental spatial context. These overlaps are not impacts.

supplemental crop overlap = Σ CROPGRIDS crop physical area inside mapped drought polygon

Progressive disclosure: Green droughts keep the compact readout focused on source severity/risk and footprint. Orange/Red may show additional asset context. A Red event opens a Human impact section, but values appear only when a reliable source provides them; any future Climate Pulse population estimate must be labelled “Estimated population exposed”.

8. Flood: source impacts and supplemental GDP context

GDACS flood records can contain structured Sendai indicators. Where a reliable source-reported human-impact metric is available, that source value takes precedence over any spatial screening.

Climate Pulse may separately calculate a country-split GDP exposure proxy from the mapped flood footprint, GHSL 2025 population and World Bank WDI 2024 GDP per capita:

GDP exposure proxy = Σcountry [GHSL population in flood footprint × WDI 2024 GDP per capita]
This is supplemental context, not economic loss. It is not a substitute for source-reported fatalities, displacement, affected population, asset damage or business interruption.

9. Reference datasets for derived fallback and context

Population fallbackJRC GHSL GHS-WUP-POP R2025A, epoch 2025, approximately 1 km.
Land / forest contextNASA MODIS MCD12C1.061, 2024, 0.05°, IGBP sub-pixel class percentages.
Crop contextFAO CROPGRIDS v1.08, 2020, 0.05°, crop physical area.
GDP contextWorld Bank WDI NY.GDP.PCAP.CD, 2024 GDP per capita in current US$.

These datasets are not allowed to overwrite an equivalent source metric silently. Their role is fallback estimation or additional context, and the UI identifies that provenance.

10. Event climate context

The public view uses annual climate context only. Climate background is extracted from the nearest valid land cell in CRU-TS v4.10, a 0.5° global land dataset covering 1901–2025. If a reported coordinate is offshore, the backend may use the nearest valid CRU land cell within 500 km and reports that distance.

DroughtTemperature + precipitation + VPD
Wildfire / HeatTemperature + precipitation + VPD
Flood / Storm / LandslideTemperature + precipitation

Monthly anomalies and near-real-time weather are not used in the current public climate panel.

11. Long-term annual indicators

Each event keeps the full 1901–2025 annual series for its selected variables. The public summary includes an early baseline, a recent ten-year mean and an ordinary least-squares long-term trend.

Early baseline1901–1930 annual mean.
Recent annual mean2016–2025 annual mean.
Long-term trendOLS slope for 1901–2025, reported per century.
Change = mean(2016–2025) − mean(1901–1930)
Trend = OLS slope(annual value ~ year) × 100 years

No significance badge or p-value is displayed. Charts retain annual values and a 5-year moving mean for readability.

12. CRU VPD calculation

CRU provides monthly actual vapour pressure (AVP) as vap. Saturation vapour pressure (SVP) is calculated from monthly mean temperature T; VPD is calculated before annual aggregation.

VPD = SVP − AVP
SVP = 6.1078 × exp(aT / (T + b))   [hPa]

For T ≥ 0°C, a = 17.269, b = 237.3; for T < 0°C, a = 21.875, b = 265.5.

Temporal-resolution limitation: monthly-mean temperature cannot reproduce the diurnal temperature cycle or sub-daily asymmetric warming. This is a long-term climate-context metric, not high-frequency VPD.

See Zhong, Z., Chen, H.W., Dai, A. et al. (2025), “Sub-diurnal asymmetric warming has amplified atmospheric dryness since the 1980s,” Nature Communications 16, 8247 ↗.

13. Climate context is not attribution

A warming, drying or VPD trend can provide useful local background information, but it does not by itself demonstrate that anthropogenic climate change caused a particular event.

Climate Pulse keeps event detection, source impact/exposure metrics, Climate Pulse-derived fallback/context, local climate context and formal event attribution as separate concepts.

14. Main limitations

  • GDACS event records, episodes, hazard polygons and impact resources can change as an event evolves.
  • GDACS/GWIS wildfire and cyclone population values are modelled exposure, not observed harm.
  • Browser footprints are simplified; Climate Pulse-derived calculations use unsimplified source geometry.
  • Forest/crop overlap does not equal ecological damage, crop failure or yield loss.
  • Gridded residential population is not real-time human presence.
  • The GDP indicator is a country-level per-capita exposure proxy and cannot represent within-country spatial economic concentration.
  • Grouped-event member sums can double-count overlapping areas or populations.
  • CRU is land-only; offshore events may use the nearest valid land cell.
  • CRU-derived VPD from monthly means omits sub-daily temperature variability.
